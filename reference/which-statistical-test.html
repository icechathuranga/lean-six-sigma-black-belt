Cheat Sheet — Which Statistical Test?

The Black Belt's most-used decision. Answer three questions, then read off the test.

Q1 — What is your output (Y) data type? Continuous (time, cost, score) or Discrete (counts, pass/fail, category)? Q2 — What is your input (X)? A group/category, or another continuous variable? Q3 — How many groups, and are they paired?

Output = Continuous

QuestionGroupsTestCourse example
Is the average different from a target?11-sample t-test—
Do two independent groups differ?22-sample t-test (Welch if variances differ)before-after-tth.csv → t=2.88, p=0.0061
Do two paired measurements differ?2 (paired)Paired t-testbefore/after on the same units
Do 3+ groups differ?3+One-way ANOVAtime-to-hire-by-region.csv → F=15.0, p=2.5e-08
Effect of 2+ categorical factors?—Two-way ANOVA / DOEdoe-factorial.csv
Does a continuous X predict Y?—Regressiontime-to-hire-regression.csv → R²=0.66
Are two continuous things related?—Correlation (r)—

If the data are badly non-normal or ordinal, use the non-parametric twin: 2 groups → Mann–Whitney U; 3+ groups → Kruskal–Wallis; paired → Wilcoxon signed-rank.

Output = Discrete / counts

QuestionTestCourse example
Are two proportions different?2-proportion test—
Is a category related to another category?Chi-square test of independenceoffer-acceptance-by-region.csv → χ²=9.18, p=0.027
Do observed counts fit expected?Chi-square goodness-of-fit—

Always

  • State H₀ (no difference / no effect) and H₁ first.
  • Pick α (usually 0.05) before looking.
  • p < α → reject H₀ (effect is real/significant). p ≥ α → insufficient evidence.
  • Check the test's assumptions (normality, equal variance, independence) and power/sample size.
  • Statistical significance ≠ practical significance. A tiny, real effect can be useless.
